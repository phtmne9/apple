Sievers' apple (*Malus sieversii*) is a rare species of wild apple and the ancestor of modern cultivated apple varieties. It is a unique natural heritage of Kazakhstan, with significant scientific, ecological, and touristic value.

The Trans-Ili Alatau region, including the area around Almaty, is considered one of the oldest centers of origin of the apple. It is here that natural populations of Sievers' apple have survived, with high genetic diversity and resistance to disease, frost, and adverse environmental conditions.

In Ile-Alatau National Park, Sievers' apple grows at elevations of 900–1,800 metres above sea level. The species is listed in the 2014 Red Book of Kazakhstan and is under state protection.

The species takes its name from the botanist Johann Sievers, who first described it during an expedition through Central Asia in the late 18th century: in 1793 he found wild apple forests in the valley of the Urdzhar river. Two centuries later, DNA analysis confirmed that this very tree is the direct ancestor of nearly all modern cultivated apple varieties.

The gene of Sievers' apple has also been found in trees growing in Germany, France, and Canada. Scientists value the species for its resistance to frost, drought, pests, and disease — making it an important resource for breeding cultivated varieties worldwide.
